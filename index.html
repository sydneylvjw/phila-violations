The City of Philadelphia’s Department of Licenses and Inspections (L&I) is responsible for ensuring that all commercial and residential structures citywide pose no threat of harm to both residents and passersby. This process is facilitated by punitive citations issued to incentivize property owners to address maintenance issues and structural failings.

Residential property safety is a significant concern for residents of Philadelphia. In addition to having some of the oldest housing stock in the nation with a median age around 30 years greater than the nation, Philadelphia is also among the most racially and economically segregated, with more than 20 percent of its residents living below the poverty line (U.S. Census, 2023).

The poorest neighborhoods in Philadelphia tend to be mostly Black, Asian or Latine, have the oldest homes, and a long history of lending discrimination when attempting to access capital to address necessary home maintenance and repairs (Escalante et al., 2024; Fairlie et al., 2022; Younkin & Kuppuswamy, 2018).

Placeholder Title 5

Each violation has an associated class and fee based on the severity of the infraction. While code violation notices (CVN) function as an administrative citation with a one-time charge, code enforcement violations (CEVs) are a form of judiciary adjudication. They can be categorized as a Basic Offense ($300 fine), a Class II Offense ($1,000 fine), or a Class III Offense ($2,000 fine). Beginning on the day a CEV is issued, the recipient receives an additional violation for every day it remains unresolved, and non-payment can result in a lien against the property to ensure payment is received, whether by monetary payment or through property dispossession and acquisition.
