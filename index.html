2: Housing Conditions

Philadelphia's housing stock is old, with a median age more than 30 years higher than the nation.

Thousands of properties around the city have been found in violation of the city's property maintenance code by Department of Licenses and Inspections (L&I).

This map shows all of the properties that L&I designated as unsafe between 2012 and 2024.

Many unsafe properties are clustered in parts of the city that have struggled to recouperate from decades of disinvestment. The concentration is particularly high in the city's West and North regions.

These communities often have majority-Black or Latine populations, and sometimes have the highest poverty rates. Poverty and residential segregation are intimately tied to housing conditions.

5: Code Violations

Some parts of the city, typically the areas with the highest poverty rates, have properties that have been determined to be an imminent danger to the neighborhood and public.
Although code enforcement can help reduce the number of vacant and nuisance properties, it does nothing to address the conditions that contribute to it:
Challenges accessing employment and municipal disinvestment that has led to property dispossession and urban infrastructure decline.
